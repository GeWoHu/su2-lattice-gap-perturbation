# Independent Referee Report — YM-05, Lemma R3'

## Objections

1. **BLOCKING — Lemma 1 mis-models a link of spin \(j\) as one symmetric block, omitting the dual endpoint block and vertex incidence.** The manuscript's Hilbert-space convention is \(L^2(G)\simeq\oplus_j V_j\otimes V_j^*\), and a flux-\(j\) link carries \(V_j\) at one endpoint and \(V_j^*\) at the other (manuscript.tex, “Peter–Weyl decomposition”). In fundamental-index language this is two \(2j\)-slot blocks, \(Sym^{2j}V\) and \(Sym^{2j}V^*\), located at opposite endpoints—not “\(2j\) \(\bar U\)-slots on link \(\ell\), fully symmetric among themselves” as R3PRIME_PROOF.md Lemma 1 asserts. In particular \(j=1\) is \(V_1\otimes V_1^*\cong 1\oplus Sym^2V\otimes Sym^2V^*\), not a single \(Sym^2\) block. The subsequent count \(u+\bar u+2j\), and hence every entry of Lemma 3's table, is therefore not derived from the actual link tensor. This is a representation-theoretic error, not a notation choice: the missing \(V_j^*\) block carries the indices at the opposite endpoint and has different epsilon/delta incidence.

2. **BLOCKING — orientation reversal is reduced to a bare \((1,1)\) slot count, but \(U^{-1}=\epsilon\bar U\epsilon^{-1}\) adds endpoint epsilon closures that can change admissibility.** Replacing a reversed link by one \(\bar U\)-slot preserves the number of fundamental slots but not the incidence pattern. The inverse identity inserts epsilon tensors at both endpoints; those tensors must be contracted with the adjacent link/vertex indices. Lemma 2 only says that “surplus \(F\)-slots ... pair with each other via \(\epsilon\)” and cites that they come from different loop factors. Different loop factors do not by themselves establish legality: the two slots must occur with compatible upper/lower incidence at a common vertex, and inverse-orientation epsilon insertions change exactly that incidence. Consequently the assertion that the \((1,1)\) overlap case is the same “singlet recoupling channel” with the same weight as \((2,0)\), merely because \(\operatorname{Tr}U^{-1}=\operatorname{Tr}U\), is unjustified at the tensor-network level.

3. **BLOCKING — Lemma 3 proves only a necessary inequality from a faulty slot model and then claims sufficiency/exhaustiveness.** The inequality \(2j\le u+\bar u\) and parity condition do not establish that the endpoint-wise singlet/triplet tensors exist for every table entry, that their recoupling weights are \(1/4,3/4\), or that no other endpoint-incidence pattern survives. Conversely, the proof gives no construction for any table entry. Thus the table is neither a complete classification of the actual tensor contractions nor a proved list of realizable channels. The theorem’s phrase “exactly” and the no-higher-spin-chain claim do not follow from Lemma 3 as written.

4. **NON-BLOCKING — the regression script does not test the disputed mathematics.** `verify_r3prime.py` checks content tables, two elementary Haar integrals, epsilon annihilation on `Sym^n` projector images, channel weights by the dimension identity \(1+3=4\), and hard-coded energy arithmetic. Its slot-count table is simply compared with the inequality already assumed in the proof. It never checks the actual \(V_j\otimes V_j^*\) link tensor, endpoint incidence, the inverse-orientation epsilon insertion, or vertex-intertwiner realizability. Thus “ALL R3' REGRESSION CHECKS PASS” is not evidence for Lemmas 1–3.

5. **NON-BLOCKING — the §2 correction of the published “three slots (odd)” sentence is directionally right but is not reconciled with the new model.** In `appendix-body.tex`, the printed proof indeed attributes two fundamental slots to the spin-1 chain of \(p\), one to \(q\), and calls the overlap “three slots (odd).” For the frozen \(|p\rangle=\chi_{1/2}(U_p)\) convention, a plaquette trace contributes one fundamental slot per traversed link, so two plaquette characters contribute two slots on an overlap link, not three. Nevertheless, the standalone proof replaces that with a single \(2j\)-slot symmetric block and never performs the endpoint accounting needed to justify the amended derivation. The correction to the literal sentence is plausible; its proposed replacement reasoning remains incomplete.

6. **NON-BLOCKING — the requested adversarial completeness check found no independent finite-lattice exception, but the offered evidence cannot prove completeness.** The geometric split \(q=p\), touching, disjoint and the listed energies are consistent with the appendix: self all-\(0\)/all-\(1\) gives \(E=0,8\); touching shared \(s=0,1\) plus six \(1/2\)'s gives \(E=9/2,13/2\); disjoint gives eight \(1/2\)'s and \(E=6\). The channel multiplicities are one at the level of the stated vertex intertwiners, and the quoted weights are \(c_0^2=1/4,c_1^2=3/4\). However, no additional assignment can be certified without correcting Objections 1–3 and then doing a complete endpoint-incidence enumeration. Brute-force passage is not a completeness proof, so the “no higher-spin chain terms” conclusion remains unverified.

## Verdict

**REJECTED.** The numerical regression script passes, and the frozen energy/weight summary is consistent with the manuscript. Nevertheless, the standalone proof does not establish R3'. Its central per-link tensor model is inconsistent with the manuscript's \(V_j\otimes V_j^*\) link decomposition: it omits the dual endpoint block and treats slot counts without endpoint incidence. The inverse-orientation identity also adds endpoint epsilon closures that the proof discards. Since Lemma 3's classification, its sufficiency claims, and the theorem's exhaustion/no-higher-spin conclusion all depend on these defects, the obstruction is blocking rather than repairable by a minor clarification. A repaired proof must model both endpoint blocks of each flux-\(j\) link, retain the epsilon insertions from reversed orientation, enumerate legal endpoint contractions, and establish both necessity and realizability of every surviving channel.

REJECTED
